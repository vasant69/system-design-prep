<SimpleDefinition>

S3 storage classes trade per-GB price against retrieval fee, retrieval latency, and minimum storage duration — cheaper classes are not simply "cheaper," they impose costs that can make them more expensive than Standard for small or frequently-accessed objects. Lifecycle policies automate moving objects between classes (transition) or deleting them (expiration) based on object age, prefix, size, or tags.

</SimpleDefinition>

## Hinglish Deep Explanation

<Hinglish>

`s3-mental-model` topic me humne dekha ki S3 ka bill 8 alag meters se banta hai. Storage class choice un meters me se teen ko directly control karta hai — storage rate, retrieval fee, aur transition charge. Ye topic un teeno ko deeply dekhta hai.

### Saare storage classes — ek table jo yaad rakhne layak hai

⚠️ Ye rates ap-south-1 (Mumbai), Aug 2026 ke hain — production budget ke liye AWS Pricing Calculator se verify karo.

| Class | Storage $/GB-mo | Min duration | Min billable size | Retrieval | Kab use karo |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Standard** | $0.025 | — | — | Free | Active data, under 30 din purana |
| **Intelligent-Tiering** | $0.025 → $0.0138 → Glacier tiers | — | — | Free (frequent/infrequent tiers) | Access pattern unknown ho |
| **Standard-IA** | $0.014 | 30 days | 128 KB | $0.01/GB | Backup, monthly reports |
| **One Zone-IA** | $0.011 | 30 days | 128 KB | $0.01/GB | Re-creatable data (thumbnails, transcoded copies) |
| **Glacier Instant Retrieval** | $0.005 | 90 days | 128 KB | $0.03/GB | Quarterly-access archives, medical images |
| **Glacier Flexible Retrieval** | $0.004 | 90 days | 40 KB | $0.01-0.03/GB + per-request | Backups, 1-5 min expedited se 5-12 hrs |
| **Glacier Deep Archive** | $0.00099 | 180 days | 40 KB | $0.02/GB | 7-saal compliance retention |
| **Express One Zone** | ~$0.11 | 1 hour | 512 KB | Free | ML training, checkpoints, hot cache |

### Asli trade-off: per-GB price poori kahani nahi hai

Interview me sabse strong signal ye bolna hai: **kabhi bhi sirf per-GB rate dekh kar storage class choose mat karo.** Teen hidden costs hain jo cheap class ko expensive bana sakte hain:

**1. Minimum billable object size.** Standard-IA me 128 KB minimum hai — matlab agar tumhare paas 10 KB ke 1 million objects hain, tum **128 KB × 1M** ke liye pay karoge, actual 10 GB ke bajaye 128 GB ke liye! Standard me 10 GB × $0.025 = $0.25 hota; IA me 128 GB × $0.014 = $1.79. IA "sasta" hone ke bajaye **7x mehnga** ho gaya. Chhote objects Standard me hi rakho, ya compact karke bade objects banao.

**2. Minimum storage duration.** Standard-IA/One Zone-IA me 30 din ka minimum hai, Glacier Instant Retrieval me 90 din, Deep Archive me 180 din — agar object usse pehle delete/overwrite/transition karo, **bacha hua duration bhi bill hoga.** Ek object jo 10 din me delete ho jaata hai IA me, phir bhi 30 din ka storage charge lagega.

**3. Retrieval fee.** Standard-IA me har GB padhne ka $0.01 lagta hai. Agar access pattern predictable nahi hai aur galti se ek bada dataset scan ho jaaye, retrieval fees storage savings se zyada ho sakti hain.

### Lifecycle policies — transition aur expiration

Lifecycle rules do cheezein karti hain: **transition** (storage class badalna) aur **expiration** (delete karna) — automatically, age/prefix/size/tag ke rules ke hisaab se.

```json
{
  "Rules": [
    {
      "ID": "ReportsArchival",
      "Status": "Enabled",
      "Filter": {
        "And": {
          "Prefix": "reports/",
          "ObjectSizeGreaterThan": 131072,
          "Tags": [{ "Key": "Retention", "Value": "long" }]
        }
      },
      "Transitions": [
        { "Days": 30, "StorageClass": "STANDARD_IA" },
        { "Days": 90, "StorageClass": "GLACIER_IR" },
        { "Days": 365, "StorageClass": "DEEP_ARCHIVE" }
      ],
      "Expiration": { "Days": 2555 }
    },
    {
      "ID": "CleanupNoncurrentVersions",
      "Status": "Enabled",
      "Filter": {},
      "NoncurrentVersionExpiration": { "NoncurrentDays": 30, "NewerNoncurrentVersions": 3 },
      "Expiration": { "ExpiredObjectDeleteMarker": true }
    },
    {
      "ID": "AbortStuckUploads",
      "Status": "Enabled",
      "Filter": {},
      "AbortIncompleteMultipartUpload": { "DaysAfterInitiation": 7 }
    }
  ]
}
```

Rules jo yaad rakhne hain: Standard se Standard-IA jaane me **minimum 30 din wait karna padta hai**; transitions hamesha ek direction me jaate hain (Standard → IA → Glacier), ulta nahi ho sakta; **har transition ka bhi per-object charge hai** (Standard-IA me ~$0.01/1,000 objects, Glacier classes me ~$0.05/1,000 — ⚠️ verify karo); lifecycle asynchronous hai, "Days: 30" ka matlab *eventually* 30 din baad hai, exact nahi; aur ek bucket me maximum **1,000 rules** allowed hain.

**Chhote objects ko Glacier bhejna paisa barbaad karta hai.** 10 KB ka object Glacier Deep Archive me bhejo to storage $0.00099 × 0.04 GB (40 KB minimum billable) = $0.00004/month, lekin transition ka one-time charge $0.00005 hai — **ek hi baar ka transition charge poore ek+ mahine ke storage se zyada hai.** Plus Glacier har object par 32-40 KB ka metadata overhead bhi charge karta hai. Isliye lifecycle rule me hamesha `ObjectSizeGreaterThan` filter lagao — typically 128 KB ya usse zyada.

Do lifecycle rules **har bucket par day-1 se honi chahiye**, chahe koi bhi archival strategy ho na ho: `AbortIncompleteMultipartUpload` (7 din baad, warna failed uploads invisible storage cost banate hain — is topic ki detail agle topic me hai) aur versioned buckets me `NoncurrentVersionExpiration` (warna purane versions hamesha ke liye bill hote rehte hain).

### Intelligent-Tiering — automatic tiering jo insurance jaisa hai

Intelligent-Tiering objects ko access pattern ke hisaab se **automatically** move karta hai — 30 din access na ho to Frequent se Infrequent Access tier ($0.0138/GB) me, 90 din me Archive Instant Access ($0.005/GB) me. Optional deep tiers bhi enable ho sakte hain (90/180 days ke Archive Access aur Deep Archive Access). Access hote hi object **turant wapas Frequent tier me** aa jaata hai, **koi retrieval fee nahi lagti** — ye Standard-IA se bada fundamental fark hai. Cost sirf ek monitoring fee: **$0.0025 per 1,000 objects per month** (128 KB se chhote objects is fee se exempt hain, lekin wo hamesha Frequent tier me rehte hain — koi saving nahi milti unhe).

**Break-even math jo interview me bolna impressive hai:** monitoring fee = $0.0000025 per object per month. Standard aur IA tier ka difference $0.025 − $0.0138 = $0.0112 per GB per month. Ek object ke liye worth hoga agar `object_size_GB × $0.0112 > $0.0000025`, jo solve karke milta hai object size > **~228 KB**. Practical rule: **average object 250 KB se bade hain to Intelligent-Tiering worth hai; chhote objects par ye ek loss hai.**

Intelligent-Tiering ka asli fayda sirf cost saving nahi, **risk removal** hai — retrieval fee na hone ki wajah se, agar access pattern kabhi unpredictable nikle (ek unexpected full-dataset scan), koi bill-shock nahi aata. Manually Standard-IA me daalna is risk ko expose karta hai; Intelligent-Tiering ise "insurance premium" ki tarah khatam kar deta hai.

</Hinglish>

## Real World Example

<RealWorld company="A Production AWS Project">

`jmfs-reports` bucket par lifecycle policy is tarah design hui: `reports/` prefix ke objects jo 128 KB se bade hain aur `Retention: long` tag rakhte hain — 30 din me Standard-IA, 90 din me Glacier Instant Retrieval, 365 din me Deep Archive, aur 7 saal (2555 din) baad expire. Chhote objects (128 KB se kam) is rule se explicitly exclude kiye gaye taaki minimum-billable-size trap na lage. Har bucket par (bina exception) do baseline rules hamesha on rakhi gayin: `AbortIncompleteMultipartUpload` 7 din, aur versioned buckets me `NoncurrentVersionExpiration` 30 din, 3 versions tak.

</RealWorld>

## Numbers That Matter

<Numbers>

- Standard: $0.025/GB-mo, no min duration/size, free retrieval
- Standard-IA: $0.014/GB-mo, 30-day min duration, 128 KB min billable size, $0.01/GB retrieval
- Glacier Instant Retrieval: $0.005/GB-mo, 90-day min, 128 KB min size, $0.03/GB retrieval
- Glacier Deep Archive: $0.00099/GB-mo, 180-day min, 40 KB min size — cheapest, but 12+ hour retrieval
- Intelligent-Tiering monitoring fee: $0.0025 per 1,000 objects/month; objects under 128 KB exempt (but no savings either)
- Intelligent-Tiering break-even: worth it only for objects roughly larger than ~228 KB
- IA "savings" trap example: 1M objects × 10 KB in Standard-IA bills as 128 GB, not 10 GB — ~7x the honest cost
- Max lifecycle rules per bucket: 1,000; Standard → Standard-IA transition requires a minimum 30-day wait

</Numbers>

## Interview Angle

<InterviewAngle>

"Kaunsi storage class use karoge aur kyun" is a near-guaranteed question — the weak answer just recites per-GB prices; the strong answer names all three hidden costs (minimum billable size, minimum duration, retrieval fee) and gives a concrete number, like the 10 KB-object-in-IA math that turns a "cheaper" class 7x more expensive. A close second: "Intelligent-Tiering hamesha use karni chahiye kya?" — the strong answer is the ~228 KB break-even math, framed as "it's insurance against unpredictable access, not free money for tiny objects." Interviewers also probe lifecycle mechanics directly — knowing transitions are one-directional and that a transition itself has a per-object charge (which can exceed a small object's monthly storage cost) is a strong signal.

</InterviewAngle>

## Common Mistakes

<Mistake>

Choosing Standard-IA or Glacier purely by comparing per-GB rate to Standard, without accounting for minimum billable object size — for objects well under 128 KB, the "cheaper" class can end up several times more expensive than Standard.

</Mistake>

<Mistake>

Transitioning small objects (a few KB) straight into Glacier Deep Archive — the one-time per-object transition charge plus the 32-40 KB metadata overhead can exceed a full month of that object's actual storage cost.

</Mistake>

<Mistake>

Deleting or transitioning an object before its storage class's minimum duration elapses (30/90/180 days) and being surprised the bill still charges for the remaining minimum-duration period.

</Mistake>

<Mistake>

Assuming Intelligent-Tiering is free money for every workload — for small objects (well under the ~228 KB break-even), the monitoring fee outweighs any tier-based savings.

</Mistake>

<Mistake>

Writing a lifecycle rule expecting a Glacier object to transition back to Standard automatically — transitions are one-directional; moving "back up" a tier requires a manual restore/copy, not a lifecycle rule.

</Mistake>

## Quick Revision

<QuickRevision>

- Per-GB price is not the whole cost story — minimum billable size (128 KB for IA classes), minimum storage duration (30-180 days), and retrieval fees can flip which class is actually cheaper
- A 10 KB object in Standard-IA bills as if it were 128 KB — small objects belong in Standard or should be compacted first
- Lifecycle rules do two things: transition (change storage class) and expiration (delete); transitions are one-directional and each transition itself has a small per-object charge
- Always filter lifecycle transitions with `ObjectSizeGreaterThan` (~128 KB) — sending tiny objects to Glacier can cost more in transition fees than it saves
- Two lifecycle rules belong on every bucket from day one: `AbortIncompleteMultipartUpload` and (if versioned) `NoncurrentVersionExpiration`
- Intelligent-Tiering charges no retrieval fee and moves objects automatically — worth it for objects roughly above ~228 KB; for smaller objects the $0.0025/1,000-object monitoring fee outweighs the savings
- Intelligent-Tiering is best framed as risk removal (insurance against unpredictable access patterns), not just a cost-saving toggle

</QuickRevision>
