## Hinglish Deep Explanation

<Hinglish>

Ye do sections mile ke bane hain — rapid-fire revision sheet (interview se 30 minute pehle padhne wali) aur red flags (jo bolte hi interviewer samajh jaata hai ki tutorial dekha hai). Dono ko ek saath rakhne ka reason simple hai: revision batata hai kya bolna hai, red flags batate hain kya nahi bolna hai — aur last-minute prep me dono equally zaroori hain.

### Master cheat-sheet — ek line me har service

Route 53: AWS ka DNS + domain registration service, 100% SLA ke saath — sabse zyada poocha jaane wala question "ALIAS aur CNAME me kya farq hai?" CloudFront: global CDN jo content ko edge locations pe cache karta hai — sabse zyada poocha jaane wala "cache hit ratio kaise improve karoge?" S3: unlimited object storage, 11 nines durability — sabse zyada poocha jaane wala "bucket private hai, CloudFront se kaise serve karoge?" Lambda: serverless compute, sirf execution time ka paisa — sabse zyada poocha jaane wala "cold start kya hai aur kaise kam karoge?" SES: managed email sending with deliverability infra — sabse zyada poocha jaane wala "SPF, DKIM, DMARC me kya farq hai?"

### One-liner instant-recall answers

Kuch sabse high-value one-liners jo bina soche bol paana chahiye: ALIAS apex pe kaam karta hai, free hai, sirf AWS resources pe; CNAME apex pe nahi chalta kyunki wahan SOA/NS records mandatory hain. Durability ka matlab hai data kho nahi jaayega (11 nines), availability ka matlab hai data abhi milega (99.99%). S3 Dec 2020 se strong read-after-write consistency deta hai, saare operations pe, bina extra cost ke. Cold start me microVM boot + runtime + package + INIT code chalta hai (100ms-2s), warm me seedha handler (1-10ms). SPF batata hai kaun bhej sakta hai (IP), DKIM prove karta hai message authentic hai (signature), DMARC batata hai fail pe kya karna hai aur alignment enforce karta hai. Sync invocation me caller wait karta hai, retry nahi hota, 6 MB limit; async me 202 turant milta hai, 2 retries, DLQ, 256 KB limit.

### Numbers jo interview se 30 minute pehle dohrane hain

Lambda: max timeout 15 min (900 sec), memory 128 MB se 10,240 MB, sync payload 6 MB, async payload 256 KB, default concurrency 1,000/region. S3: max object size 5 TB, durability 11 nines, per-prefix throughput 3,500 write / 5,500 read per second, presigned URL max expiry 7 din. Route 53: hosted zone $0.50/month, standard query $0.40 per million, health check interval 30 sec (threshold 3). CloudFront: default TTL 24h (max 1 saal), free invalidations 1,000 paths/month, 700+ edge locations, ACM cert region us-east-1 mandatory. SES: sandbox quota 200 emails/24h, bounce rate danger 10% (review at 5%), complaint rate danger 0.5% (review at 0.1%), pricing $0.10 per 1,000 emails.

### Last-minute confidence lines

Paanch lines jo kisi bhi answer me fit ho jaati hain aur depth dikhati hain: "Maine ALIAS use kiya CNAME nahi, kyunki zone apex pe CNAME allowed nahi hai aur AWS resources pe alias queries free hain." "Maine bucket poori tarah private rakha OAC ke saath, aur bucket policy me SourceArn condition lagayi taaki confused deputy problem na ho." "Main invalidation ki jagah content hashing use karta hoon — versioning free hai, instant hai, aur rollback bhi easy ho jaata hai." "File uploads Lambda ke through nahi jaate — 6 MB payload limit hai aur compute pe paisa dena waste hai. Presigned URL se browser directly S3 pe upload karta hai." "SES me sirf DKIM kaafi nahi tha — maine custom MAIL FROM domain bhi configure kiya taaki SPF alignment DMARC ke liye pass ho."

### General red flags — ye mat bolo, iski jagah ye bolo

Kuch sabse damaging statements aur unke strong replacements: "Maine bucket public kar diya taaki website chale" ki jagah "Maine bucket private rakha aur CloudFront ko OAC se access diya, taaki koi CDN bypass na kar sake." "AWS free tier me tha isliye use kiya" ki jagah "Maine cost model evaluate kiya — is traffic pattern pe serverless ka idle cost zero hai." "Deploy ke baad main `/*` invalidate kar deta hoon" ki jagah "Mera build content hashing karta hai, to main sirf index.html invalidate karta hoon." "DNS propagate hone me 24-48 ghante lagte hain" ki jagah "Route 53 me change 60 second me global ho jaata hai; delay downstream resolvers ki cached entries ka hota hai." "S3 eventually consistent hai" ki jagah "Dec 2020 se S3 strong read-after-write consistency deta hai." "VPC me Lambda daalne se cold start bahut badh jaata hai" ki jagah "Ye 2019 se pehle sach tha; Hyperplane ENI ke baad ye penalty practically negligible hai." "Maine `s3:*` permission de di taaki kaam ho jaaye" ki jagah "Maine specific actions aur specific prefix-level ARNs diye."

### Behavioural red flags — kaise bolte ho, sirf kya bolte ho nahi

Sabse bada behavioural red flag hai **confidently galat bolna**. Agar nahi pata to bolo: "Sir, isko maine apne project me use nahi kiya. Mera understanding ye hai ki X, but main confident nahi hoon — main isko padhunga." Ye honesty seniority ka signal hai — galat answer confidently dena ka matlab hai ki production me bhi galat decision confidently loge. Doosra red flag hai sirf "what" batana, "why" nahi — "CloudFront ek CDN hai" definition hai, "maine CloudFront isliye lagaya kyunki S3 website endpoint HTTPS support nahi karta" reasoning hai, aur interviewer reasoning sunna chahta hai. Teesra, har cheez pe "yes" bolna — agar multi-region setup nahi kiya to seedha "nahi sir, mera scale usko justify nahi karta tha, but agar karna hota to main latency-based routing use karta" bolna zyada strong hai jhooth bolne se. Chautha, trade-off ka zikr na karna — sirf fayde batana bina nuksaan ke lagta hai ki socha hi nahi. Paanchva, numbers na bolna — "S3 bahut durable hai" ke bajaye "S3 11 nines durability deta hai" bolna clearly better hai.

### Golden phrases jo naturally use karne se answers senior lagte hain

"Iska trade-off ye hai ki..." — "Maine X consider kiya tha lekin Y ki wajah se reject kiya." — "Ye scale pe change ho jaayega — abhi mere volume pe ye sahi hai, lekin agar traffic 10x ho to main..." — "Blast radius contain karne ke liye maine..." — "Ye defence in depth hai — ek layer fail bhi ho to doosri bachaayegi." — "Main ye assume nahi karta, main isko metric se measure karta hoon." — "Main honest rahunga — ye maine implement nahi kiya, but approach ye hoti."

### Final checklist — interview se pehle

30-second aur 2-minute pitch aawaz me practice kiya (mann me nahi), numbers ek baar dekhe, har service ka "maine X kyun choose kiya" ready hai, har service ka "maine Y kyun reject kiya" ready hai, apne project ka approximate monthly cost pata hai, ek cheez ready hai jo galat hui thi aur kaise fix ki, ek cheez ready hai jo agli baar alag karte, aur interviewer ke liye 2 sawaal ready hain.

</Hinglish>

## Interview Angle

<InterviewAngle>

This topic sits slightly apart from the rest — it isn't testing a new concept, it's testing recall speed and self-awareness under pressure. Interviewers notice when a candidate can produce a precise number instantly ("11 nines," "6 MB," "us-east-1") versus when they hedge and approximate everything, and precision reads as genuine hands-on familiarity rather than surface-level reading. Equally, interviewers are trained to notice the behavioral pattern of confident wrongness far more than a gap in knowledge — admitting "I haven't used this in production, here's my understanding" is treated as a positive signal, not a negative one, because it predicts how the candidate will behave when they hit an unfamiliar problem on the job.

</InterviewAngle>

## Common Mistakes

<Mistake>

Confidently guessing instead of admitting the limit of your knowledge — this is the single most damaging red flag, because it signals that the same overconfidence would show up in a production decision, not just in an interview answer.

</Mistake>

<Mistake>

Repeating "DNS propagation takes 24-48 hours" — this is one of the most common tutorial-sourced myths; the correct framing is that Route 53 itself propagates in ~60 seconds and the apparent delay is downstream resolver cache expiry (TTL).

</Mistake>

<Mistake>

Answering "yes" to every capability question even when the true answer is "no, and here's why that was the right call for this scale" — false confidence is weaker than an honest, reasoned "no."

</Mistake>

<Mistake>

Describing only benefits and never trade-offs for a chosen service — every real technical decision has a downside, and omitting it reads as not having thought the decision through.

</Mistake>

<Mistake>

Giving vague, number-free answers ("S3 is very durable," "it's pretty cheap") when a precise number was available and would have been far more convincing ("11 nines durability," "about $1/month").

</Mistake>

## Quick Revision

<QuickRevision>

- Master one-liners: Route 53 = DNS + 100% SLA; CloudFront = edge cache; S3 = 11 nines durability; Lambda = pay-per-ms compute; SES = managed deliverability infra
- Five last-minute confidence lines to have ready: ALIAS-not-CNAME, OAC + SourceArn, content hashing over invalidation, presigned URLs for uploads, custom MAIL FROM for SPF alignment
- The single biggest behavioral red flag: confidently giving a wrong answer instead of saying "I haven't used this in production, but my understanding is..."
- "DNS propagation takes 24-48 hours" is the most common myth to avoid repeating — Route 53 itself propagates in ~60 seconds
- Always pair a benefit with its trade-off — stating only the upside of a decision reads as not having considered the downside
- Numbers beat adjectives: "11 nines" and "~$1/month" are more convincing than "very durable" and "pretty cheap"
- Final pre-interview checklist: practiced pitch out loud, numbers reviewed, "why X" and "why not Y" ready for every service, own project's monthly cost known, one real failure and fix ready, two questions for the interviewer ready

</QuickRevision>
