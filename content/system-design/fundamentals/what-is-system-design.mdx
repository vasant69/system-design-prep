<SimpleDefinition>

System design is the process of defining the architecture, components, data flow, and interfaces of a software system to satisfy specified functional and non-functional requirements — such as scale, latency, availability, and cost. A system design interview evaluates how you **reason** through ambiguous, large-scale engineering problems, not whether you memorize a specific architecture.

</SimpleDefinition>

## Hinglish Deep Explanation

<Hinglish>

Dekho, sabse pehli galti jo log karte hain — woh sochte hain ki system design ek "syllabus" hai. Jaise DSA mein tumne arrays, trees, graphs cover kiye, waise hi system design mein "load balancer padh liya, caching padh li, ho gaya." Yeh mindset galat hai, aur yehi wajah hai ki log 6 mahine prep karke bhi interview mein flat ho jaate hain.

System design asal mein ek **engineering conversation** hai. Interviewer tumhe ek vague problem dega — jaise "design WhatsApp" ya "design a URL shortener" — aur dekhega ki tum us ambiguity ko kaise handle karte ho. .NET Core background se aane wale logon ke liye yeh especially important hai samajhna: tumhara coding skill already strong hai (LeetCode wagera), lekin system design round ek bilkul different muscle test karta hai — **judgment**, not just correctness.

### Interview mein evaluate kya hota hai

Har company ka rubric thoda different hota hai, lekin roughly yeh 5 dimensions har jagah common hain:

1. **Requirements gathering** — Kya tumne solution jump karne se pehle sahi sawaal poochhe? "Kitne users hain? Read-heavy hai ya write-heavy? Consistency chahiye ya availability?" Agar tum seedha diagram banana shuru kar do, red flag hai.
2. **Structured thinking** — Kya tumhara approach organized hai, ya tum random components (Kafka! Redis! Kubernetes!) throw kar rahe ho bina reason ke? Interviewer dekhta hai ki tumhare paas ek **framework** hai ya nahi.
3. **Trade-off reasoning** — Yeh sabse important hai aur sabse zyada log yahin fail hote hain. System design mein koi "perfect" answer nahi hota. Har decision ka ek cost hota hai. Agar tum bolte ho "hum SQL use karenge" bina yeh bataye ki NoSQL kyun nahi, to lagta hai tumne bas ek keyword cram kiya hai.
4. **Depth on follow-up** — Interviewer tumhare kisi bhi component ko "zoom in" kar sakta hai. Bola "hum caching use karenge" — ab agla sawaal aayega "cache invalidation kaise karoge? Thundering herd ka kya hoga agar cache cold start ho?" Agar yahan tumhara jawab sirf "hum TTL set kar denge" hai, depth kam lagegi.
5. **Communication** — Kya tum apna thought process bol rahe ho ya chup chaap diagram bana rahe ho? Interviewer tumhare **dimaag ke andar** dekhna chahta hai, final answer nahi, uska raasta dekhna chahta hai.

Ek senior engineer jab interview leta hai, woh **signal** dhundta hai, not keywords. Agar tum "CAP theorem" bol dete ho lekin explain nahi kar pate ki tumhare specific system mein CP kyun choose kiya ya AP kyun — woh keyword tumhe koi marks nahi dilata, ulta suspicious lagta hai ki tum ratke aaye ho.

### Ek mental framework jo kaam karta hai

Zyadatar strong candidates (chahe unhe pata ho ya na ho) is flow ko follow karte hain — isko yaad rakhne ke liye ek simple acronym use karo: **RADIO**.

- **R — Requirements**: Functional (kya karna hai) aur non-functional (kitna fast, kitna reliable, kitna scale) dono clarify karo.
- **A — Architecture / API**: High level components aur unke beech ka contract (API endpoints) define karo.
- **D — Data model**: Kaunsa data store, kaunsa schema, kya relationships hain.
- **I — Interface deep dive**: Ek ya do critical flows ko detail mein trace karo (request se response tak).
- **O — Optimize & bottlenecks**: Scale badhao, failure points identify karo, trade-offs discuss karo.

Yeh koi rigid rule nahi hai — interviewer beech mein tumhe kahin bhi le ja sakta hai. Lekin agar tumhare paas yeh mental skeleton hai, to tum kabhi "blank" nahi hoge. Jab confusion ho, wapas is framework pe aa jao: "Okay, requirements clear hain, ab main high-level architecture propose karta hoon..."

### Kab kya depth chahiye

Yeh bhi samajhna zaroori hai — interview ka **level** depth decide karta hai. SDE-1/2 round mein interviewer zyada expect karta hai ki tum ek reasonable design bana lo aur basic trade-offs samjho. Senior/Staff level pe expectation badal jaati hai — wahan woh dekhna chahte hain ki tum **organizational** trade-offs bhi samajhte ho: team ownership boundaries, migration cost, operational complexity, on-call burden. Ek fresher jo bolta hai "hum microservices use karenge kyunki scalable hai" — yeh junior jawab hai. Ek senior jo bolta hai "hum monolith se start karenge kyunki abhi team chhoti hai aur microservices ka operational overhead abhi justify nahi hota" — yeh senior-level judgment hai.

</Hinglish>

## Real World Example

<RealWorld company="Amazon">

Amazon ke system design interviews (aksar "bar raiser" round ke saath) specifically **ambiguity handling** test karte hain. Unka official guidance hi yeh hai ki candidate "dive deep" leadership principle dikhaye — matlab ek do components sirf name-drop mat karo, kam se kam ek do areas mein bahut gehraai tak jaao (failure modes, capacity numbers, specific algorithm choice).

</RealWorld>

<RealWorld company="Flipkart / Swiggy">

Indian product companies jaise Flipkart aur Swiggy mein system design rounds aksar **operational reality** pe focus karte hain — jaise "Big Billion Days" ya "New Year's Eve" jaisi traffic spikes ke liye design kaise adapt hota hai. Yeh generic FAANG-style "design Twitter" se thoda alag hai — yahan interviewer tumse pooch sakta hai "agar Diwali sale mein traffic 50x ho jaaye achanak, tumhara design kaha break hoga?" Iska matlab hai ki tumhe apne design ke weak points khud identify karne aane chahiye, defensive hoke sirf "yeh perfect hai" bolne ke bajaye.

</RealWorld>

## Numbers That Matter

<Numbers>

- Typical system design round length — 45–60 minutes
- Time you should spend on requirements gathering — 5–10 minutes (not more, not less)
- Time on high-level design before deep dive — ~15 minutes
- Rounds in a full loop that are usually system design — 1–2 out of 4–6 total rounds
- Common scale anchor used in Indian-context problems — 10M DAU, ~1000–5000 RPS average, 10x peak
- Follow-up "what if scale increases 10x" — asked in the majority of senior-level rounds

</Numbers>

## Interview Angle

<InterviewAngle>

Interviewer ka sabse bada dar yeh hota hai ki candidate **silent** ho jaaye ya **overconfident** solution de de bina reasoning ke. Isliye jab bhi tumse "design X" bola jaaye, seedha whiteboard pe mat kudo. Model answer structure kuch aisa hoga:

"Design karne se pehle main kuch cheezein clarify karna chahunga — [functional requirements 2-3 sawaal], aur scale ke baare mein — [DAU, read/write ratio, latency expectation]. Iske basis pe main pehle ek simple high-level design propose karunga, phir critical path deep dive karunga, aur end mein bottlenecks aur unke trade-offs discuss karunga."

Yeh ek line hi tumhe pehle 30 second mein "structured thinker" ka signal de deti hai — jo baaki interview ke liye tumhare favor mein kaam karta hai.

</InterviewAngle>

## Common Mistakes

<Mistake>

Seedha solution design karna shuru kar dena bina requirements clarify kiye — interviewer ko lagta hai tumne bina samjhe hi answer rat liya hai.

</Mistake>

<Mistake>

Har buzzword throw karna ("hum Kafka, Redis, Kubernetes, aur GraphQL use karenge") bina yeh bataye ki har piece **kyun** zaroori hai us specific problem ke liye.

</Mistake>

<Mistake>

Sirf "yeh scale karega" bolna without numbers. "Scalable" ek meaningless word hai jab tak tum bata na do — kitna scale, kaise scale, kis cost pe.

</Mistake>

<Mistake>

Trade-offs discuss na karna. Agar tumne SQL choose kiya, interviewer ka agla sawaal hoga "NoSQL kyun nahi?" — agar tumhare paas jawab nahi hai to lagta hai tumne guess kiya tha.

</Mistake>

<Mistake>

Chup chaap sochna. Interview mein silence tumhare against jaata hai — apna thought process zor se bolo, galat direction mein bhi jaa rahe ho to interviewer usse course-correct kar dega, lekin tabhi jab woh tumhara thinking sun paaye.

</Mistake>

## Quick Revision

<QuickRevision>

- System design interview = judgment test, not architecture trivia
- 5 evaluation dimensions: requirements gathering, structured thinking, trade-off reasoning, depth on follow-up, communication
- Use RADIO: Requirements → Architecture/API → Data model → Interface deep dive → Optimize
- Spend 5-10 min clarifying requirements before designing anything
- Har decision ka trade-off bolo — "yeh use karenge kyunki X, cost yeh hai Y"
- Senior level = organizational trade-offs bhi matter karte hain, not just technical
- Silence is the enemy — thought process zor se bolo
- Indian companies (Flipkart/Swiggy) often test spike-handling (sale-day traffic), not just steady-state design

</QuickRevision>
